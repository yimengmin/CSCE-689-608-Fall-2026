## Course Description

This graduate-level course introduces modern machine learning and generative
AI techniques for scientific discovery.

Topics include graph neural networks, generative models including diffusion
and flow matching, and foundation models for AI for Science.

The course emphasizes applications in materials science, chemistry, and
molecular design, including molecular and crystal structure generation,
inverse design, and scientific data modeling.

Students will read and discuss recent research papers, implement modern
machine learning methods, and complete a research-oriented course project.

---

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

1. Explain how modern machine learning and generative AI techniques can be
   applied to scientific discovery problems in materials science, chemistry,
   and molecular design.

2. Develop and implement machine learning models for scientific applications.

3. Critically evaluate recent research literature in AI for Science and
   generative modeling.

4. Design and complete a research-oriented project using modern machine
   learning and generative AI methods.

---

## Course Format

The course will be conducted primarily as a **research-oriented seminar**.

Class activities will include:

- Lectures on modern machine learning methods for scientific discovery
- Reading and discussion of recent research papers
- Student paper presentations
- Guest lectures and emerging research topics
- Programming and computational experiments
- Development of a research-oriented course project

There is **no midterm or final exam**.

Students are expected to keep pace with assigned readings, participate in
class discussions, and begin developing their course projects early in the
semester.

## Programming Assignments

Students will complete two programming assignments involving the
implementation and evaluation of machine learning methods introduced in the
course.

Assignments may involve molecular, chemical, materials, or other scientific
datasets and are intended to provide hands-on experience with modern machine
learning methods for scientific applications.

- **Programming Assignment 1:** Due Friday of Week 7
- **Programming Assignment 2:** Due Friday of Week 10

---

## Literature Review

Students will complete a critical review of recent research on a selected
AI for Science topic.

The literature review should:

- Identify important recent papers
- Explain the main methodological ideas
- Compare the strengths and limitations of different approaches
- Identify open research questions and directions
- Help motivate and scope the final course project

**Due:** Week 9

---

## Course Project

A major component of the course is a research-oriented final project.

Students may:

1. Reproduce results from a recent AI for Science paper;
2. Apply an existing method to a new scientific problem or dataset; or
3. Develop and evaluate a new methodological idea.

Possible project areas include:

- Graph neural networks for scientific data
- Diffusion models
- Flow matching
- Foundation models for science

Students will present their work during the final weeks of the semester and
submit a written final report.

---

## Computing Resources

Several components of this course involve computational experiments with
modern machine learning models.

Students may use GPU-accelerated computing resources for:

- Training and evaluating graph neural networks
- Training and evaluating diffusion models
- Flow matching experiments
- Fine-tuning and evaluating foundation models
- Reproducing computational results from recent research papers
- Conducting experiments for the final course project

Cloud and/or university computing resources may be made available to support
course assignments and research projects.

Students are expected to write reproducible code and appropriately document
the computational resources used in their experiments.
